### Independent Study

#### Problem Statement
As more renewable resources are integrated into the electrical grid, the control models which previously 
relied on characteristics of synchronous generators must be reevaluated to ensure continued stability of 
the system. My goal will be to construct grid systems of interest and to develop tests which evaluate 
selected control models and parameters.

#### Discussion of Prior Work
The electrical grid requires a precise balancing of generation and load as well as synchronization of 
frequency, all of which are stabilized through various control systems. Work by Milano, et al [1] provides 
background information on the effect of replacing traditional power generation sources with renewable 
resources on these control systems and presents open questions. Some of the effects and open 
questions relevant to this Independent Study include; decreased window of time during which a control 
system must react to stabilize the grid after a disturbance as well as balancing precision and compute in 
selecting a modeling fidelity.  Next, in [2], the focus is on outlining the existing control models for 
traditional and renewable energy sources. Most importantly, [2] describes a control paradigm that could 
provide the stability that is lost when traditional power generation sources are replaced with renewable 
resources and proposes control systems as a unifying tool to integrate the complexities of this system 
moving forward. Finally, [3] introduces an open-source software package used for running custom 
simulations to test grid stability.
